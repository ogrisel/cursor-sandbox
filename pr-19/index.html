Shared payment-platform fraud simulator

An educational, fast-running simulator of card-not-present payments on a payment platform shared by 40 online shops. A legacy rule engine is already deployed, and labels are delayed, incomplete, noisy and censored by that engine's own decisions. On top of the simulator, a skrub + HistGradientBoostingClassifier pipeline with point-in-time window features is backtested on calendar folds, and evaluated in euros against the private ground truth that only a simulation provides.

Main findings (seed 0, 4 × 21-day test windows)

Do window aggregates matter?Yes. AP 0.43 → 0.72 for the censored model (legacy rule score alone: 0.11).003

What does back-dating labels in features do?It wins offline in 2 of 4 folds and loses 0.07–0.23 AP once served.003

How much money does censoring cost?Nothing measurable: +€32.0k vs +€31.8k uplift over the legacy rules (67% of the clairvoyant uplift for both).004

Iterations

  • 001 — first simulator, causal features and backtest (superseded: the problem was trivially separable)
  • 002 — critical review, mechanistic v2 simulator, difficulty check
  • 003 — skrub DataOps, lag-aware label features and leakage
  • 004 — business evaluation, evaluation bias and calibration
