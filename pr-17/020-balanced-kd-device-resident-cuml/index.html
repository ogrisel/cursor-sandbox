020 — balanced clusters and features, with device-resident cuML input

The previous five-task mix pairs a large number of clusters with very few features, or a wide matrix with very few clusters. This iteration adds k256-d128: 150,000 float32 Gaussian rows, 128 features, and 256 centers. Its n * k * d is 4.92 billion, comparable to k100-d32 (4.80 billion), but both the centroid count and the feature width are substantial.

The task is calibrated against stock scikit-learn before the full run. One greedy k-means++ fit with 12 Lloyd iterations takes 6.60 s, comfortably under the 60 s worker limit. The full result set is results.json.

The benchmark driver now resolves its own Pixi interpreter rather than assuming the checkout is at ~/kmeans_lloyd. It also changes the cuML contract: the timed region now starts only after the samples and the shared-random-row centers are CuPy arrays on the NVIDIA L4. output_type="cupy" keeps the fitted centers on the device, and their transfer for the host-side inertia check happens after timing. This matches the existing PyTorch and CuPy array-API paths. The relevant code is _worker_cuml and _pythons.

Setup

  • One host: four CPU threads and one NVIDIA L4. Float32, TF32 disabled, three seeds, greedy k-means++ followed by at most 12 Lloyd iterations, and tol=1e-8.
  • CPU contenders are stock scikit-learn 1.7.2 and cakedev0's scikit-learn 1.10.dev0 build at 7e26a5c. GPU contenders are eager array API on PyTorch CUDA and CuPy CUDA, plus cuML 26.10.00.
  • “Native” uses index_add_ for PyTorch or add.at for CuPy. “Generic” forces the portable one-hot update. At k=256, d=128, both CUDA backends dispatch to the native update.
  • The first CuPy-native and cuML process paid one-time initialization costs (10.2 s and 3.6 s). Their seed-0 cells were rerun with a warm cache and replace those values in results.json.

Quality and memory

All greedy full fits end between 118.919 and 118.930 inertia per point. The shared-start values agree within 0.0005, around 118.960. cuML scalable is faster but finishes at 119.708: 0.66% above the best greedy result. This is consistent with the large-k scalable-initializer behavior in 011.

The input occupies about 73 MiB. Every array-API full fit peaks around 147 MiB above its allocator baseline; stock scikit-learn and cakedev0 peak around 146 MiB above VmHWM. The cuML nvidia-smi increments are 14 MiB for greedy and 12 MiB for scalable. These meters are not directly comparable across host and device allocators, but the cuML figures exclude the pre-existing device input by construction.

Conclusion

k256-d128 fills the missing balanced region without threatening the time limit. It confirms that native accumulation helps on CPU and remains a small GPU win, while distinguishing the k-means++ advantage of cakedev0 from its Lloyd kernel. It also changes the cuML comparison to a device-to-device one: cuML is the fastest shared-start Lloyd kernel here, CuPy narrowly wins against cuML greedy for the complete greedy fit, and cuML scalable is fastest overall at a 0.66% objective cost.
