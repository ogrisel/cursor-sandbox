Lloyd k-means in the Python array API

What this PR explores

This experiment studies nearest-centroid assignment, Lloyd updates, empty clusters, and centroid initialization through the Python array API. The eager implementation runs with PyTorch on CPU, CUDA, and XPU, with CuPy on CUDA, and with dpnp on a Level Zero GPU. Comparisons cover stock scikit-learn, scikit-learn with k-means++ performance patches, Cython, and cuML.

The journal progresses from kernel-fusion probes to bounded-memory tiled assignment, full fits, repeated-seed and shared-start comparisons, empty-center relocation, and greedy versus scalable initialization. The current six-task mix spans 3 to 3000 clusters, 2 to 1000 features, and 135000 to 2100000 samples, including a balanced 256-cluster, 128-feature case. Runs use float32, eager execution, TF32 disabled, three seeds, and a 60-second worker limit.

Latest results

023 adds the portable array-API path on dpnp GPU arrays to the 022 Arc B390 measurements. 022 holds PyTorch XPU (native and one-hot), PyTorch CPU, stock scikit-learn 1.9.1, and cakedev0. CuPy and cuML are absent on this host.

Main conclusions:

  • dpnp, which always uses the one-hot update, is faster than native Torch CPU on every full fit. It is also faster than the XPU one-hot update at 17 clusters (2.57 s against 2.83 s) and at 256 (1.11 s against 1.87 s).
  • The XPU one-hot update remains the fastest full fit at 3 clusters (3.22 s; dpnp is 4.83 s). From 17 clusters up, cakedev0 is still the fastest full fit. dpnp is second at 17 clusters (2.57 s against 2.35 s) and at 256 (1.11 s against 1.06 s).
  • At 256 clusters dpnp Lloyd takes 0.47 s, level with stock scikit-learn and cakedev0. At 550 and 3000 clusters both XPU updates and Cython Lloyd are faster than dpnp.
  • On Arc B390 the one-hot PyTorch update is faster than index_add_ through 256 clusters (022). The CUDA few-cluster cutoff would keep index_add_ on k17-d180, where the one-hot update is the faster XPU path.
